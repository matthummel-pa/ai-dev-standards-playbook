# Business operations

## 1. What this covers
* Client proposals, discovery questionnaires, project scopes (SOWs), technical documentation, client communication templates, and strategic planning.
* Stay pragmatic and solution-oriented. Anticipate bottlenecks, draft professional follow-ups, and translate technical concepts into plain business terms.

## 2. Proposals and scopes
* Every scope lists deliverables, what's out of scope, timeline, price, payment terms, and what the owner needs from the client.
* Custom quotes. No menu of add-ons.
* Simple sites run two to three weeks; custom fields or booking run four to eight.
* Any request outside the written scope gets a short change note with cost and timeline impact before work starts.
* Don't state a price, rate, or discount on the owner's behalf. Draft it and leave the number for them to confirm.

## 3. Client communication
* Lead with the decision or the ask. Put the detail underneath.
* No jargon without a plain-language explanation.
* Bad news goes out early, with the cause, the fix, and the new date.
* Anything sent in the owner's name is a draft until he approves it.

## 4. Lead handling
* For a poor fit, send a short, kind no and a referral when one exists.
* Upwork and cold outreach copy follows `marketing-copy.md` and leads with the prospect's problem.

## 5. Pre-launch checklist
* Forms tested end to end, including the notification email.
* 301 redirects in place for every changed URL; no broken internal links.
* Analytics and consent verified; key events firing.
* `noindex` removed; sitemap submitted to Google and Bing.
* SSL valid, mixed content cleared, favicon and Open Graph image set.
* Caching and backups configured and tested.
* Accessibility pass complete; 404 page in place.
* Editor-role walkthrough of the most common content updates.

## 6. Handoff
* Accounts for hosting, DNS, and the Git repo sit under the client's name.
* Deliver a plain-language admin guide covering the edits the client will make most.
* Deliver deploy notes for the next developer.
* Remove the developer's access unless the client asks to keep it.

## 7. Privacy and compliance
* Collect only the data a form needs, and say what happens to it next to the form.
* Consent before non-essential cookies and tracking, with consent mode v2 wired to the banner.
* Keep the privacy policy accurate to the tools actually installed.
* Newsletter sign-ups are opt-in, with an unsubscribe link on every send.
* Publish an accessibility statement and keep it current.

## 8. Documentation
* Every repo has a README covering setup, build, deploy, and where content is edited.
* Write docs for the next developer and the site owner as two separate audiences.
